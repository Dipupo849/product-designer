Scope of this document

What every image here actually is.

The plates in stage 05 and 06 are real renders. They were path-traced in Blender Cycles — physically based materials, global illumination, real depth of field, real contact shadows. They are not photographs, and no part of this page is an AI-generated image.

The geometry behind them is a reconstruction. It was rebuilt from the 3ds Max and Fusion 360 references to the dimensions recorded in this dossier — it is not the original 1.03 M-quad asset or the original parametric solid. Silhouette, proportion, component placement and every feature are carried across from the source; nothing was added to make a render more attractive. The clay comparisons in stage 05 show the same model with its materials stripped, at the same camera.

The engine is not the one in the brief. Stage 05's specification was written for V-Ray or Corona against PhysCamera002. The plates on this page were rendered in Cycles, so read those settings as the intent and the result as the Cycles equivalent.

Everything outside stages 05 and 06 remains hand-authored vector drawing — which is why it carries tolerances, section cuts and callouts a render cannot. Dimensions are marked reconstructed throughout: proportionally derived and internally consistent, not measured off the original model. Substitute the real parameter values before releasing anything for manufacture.

01
Stage 01

Design Concept

Two objects with nothing in common except the discipline. One resolves a mass problem in upholstery; the other resolves a safety problem in 2.5 mm of moulded wall. The brief for both was to let the construction be the ornament.

Project 01 · Furniture

COUCH — 2.5-seat leather sofa

The governing idea is a single continuous rail: the back crown falls without a break into both arms and terminates at the same height on each side. There is no separate arm component, no visible join, and no vertical seam anywhere on the outside back. The entire mass reads as one skin stretched over one form.

That mass then has to look light. It does so by sitting on four splayed tapered legs that lift the frame 260 mm clear of the floor, so the shadow gap runs uninterrupted from end to end. Everything heavy is above the rail line; everything below it is thin.

Button tufting is structural rather than decorative. Eleven self-covered buttons at 150 mm pitch pull the back panel against the foam's return and hold the crown's curve under load — without them the top rail flattens as soon as someone leans back.

  • No visible fastener, staple or seam on any outside face
  • Seat height held at 430 mm for dining-adjacent use
  • Leg splay symmetric on both axes, so the object has no bad angle
  • Cover cut so the hide's growth direction runs along the rail
Project 02 · Instrument

PARAMETER — transformer monitor enclosure

A bench instrument that puts 230 VAC metering behind a shell you would be happy to leave on a desk. The whole front face carries exactly two features: one window and one knob. Nothing else is allowed to break that plane — no fasteners, no labels, no LEDs.

All four lid screws are therefore driven from the top through counterbored bosses, and every cable entry is on the rear. The knob is the only moving part a user touches, and it selects one of six transformer taps.

Because the box contains a live mains section, the internal layout is the design. A single straight boundary divides the board: hazardous on one side, SELV on the other, with the only crossing being the PZEM-004T's optically isolated serial link. The shell's job is to make that boundary impossible to defeat by accident.

  • Front face restricted to one window, one knob
  • Mains and SELV sections separated by a straight, unbroken line
  • Lid removable without disturbing any mains conductor
  • Wall section constant at 2.5 mm for even shrink

03
Stage 03

3D Modelling

The reference file reports 1,033,680 polygons and 1,033,736 vertices. Those two numbers are not decoration — read together they describe how the mesh is built, and they are the first thing to check before a model is trusted for print.

Mesh audit COUCH · reading the counters Source: 3ds Max statistics overlay
What the difference proves

V − F = 56

1,033,736 − 1,033,680. For a closed, all-quad surface every face carries four edges and every edge is shared by two faces, so E = 2F and Euler's relation V − E + F = 2 collapses to V = F + 2 per shell.

56 ÷ 2 = 28 shells

The counters are consistent with exactly 28 separate closed shells, all quads, no open borders, no triangles and no n-gons anywhere in a million-polygon model. A single stray hole or triangulated cap would break the arithmetic. This is the cheapest watertightness check available and it costs one subtraction.

Shell inventory · one consistent reading
  • Outer rail — back and both arms, one skin1
  • Inside back panel1
  • Seat cushion1
  • Welt cord — top rail, seat front2
  • Self-covered buttons11
  • Legs4
  • Leg ferrules4
  • Floor glides4
  • Total closed shells28

One allocation that satisfies the count. Other splits are possible — the arithmetic fixes the total at 28, not which parts they are.

Subdivision

Where a million quads comes from

Catmull–Clark quadruples face count per iteration. A cage small enough to stay editable in the viewport reaches render density in three passes — which is why the file runs at 16 FPS with the modifier live rather than collapsed.

One number that does not divide

1,033,680 ÷ 4³ = 16,151.25 — not an integer. The render mesh is therefore not a single uniform three-iteration pass over one cage. At least one shell group runs at a different iteration count; carrying the small hardware — buttons, ferrules, glides — at iterate 2 while the upholstery runs at iterate 3 satisfies the total and is the sensible choice anyway, since a Ø24 button gains nothing from 64× density. Worth confirming against the modifier stack before the file is archived.

Finish note

Both objects are specified in the single finish shown in the source files — black aniline on the sofa, RAL 9001 on the enclosure. The material graphs are parameterised so an alternate colourway is a value change rather than a re-build, but no alternates are proposed here: the geometry was designed around these two finishes and re-specifying them is a separate brief.
